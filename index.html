Research

My work sits between international macroeconomics and development economics. Across both strands the organising question is the same: when a shock arrives — a conflict, a productivity gain — what determines whether an economy can actually reallocate in response, and who bears the cost when it cannot?

The first strand measures geopolitical risk and traces how it transmits to trade and prices in Asia. I construct monthly risk indices from domestic-language newspapers across seventeen economies, which shows that locally perceived risk diverges systematically from global English-language measures — and that the identifiable price signal sits in the local coverage rather than the Anglosphere benchmark. A companion paper finds that trade fragments along geopolitical alignment gradients, with China-salient risk generating reallocation more than three times larger than US-salient risk, and ASEAN operating as a partial regional buffer.

The second strand asks why structural transformation in India has been both slow and skewed. Using NSS and IHDS microdata, I document that inherited sectoral attachment is strong and sharply stratified by education, and that within-career mobility only partly undoes it. Embedding these two margins in a multi-sector overlapping-generations model shows that identical productivity growth can produce very different reallocation paths: structural change is constrained not only by whether workers leave agriculture, but by where they are able to go.

Methodologically I work with text analysis, structural gravity estimation, panel local projections, and quantitative macro models calibrated to microdata.

Working Papers

Where You Start, Where You Stay: Career Frictions and Structural Change in India
Deeksha Kokas
Working paper
Inherited sectoral attachment and life-cycle mobility frictions reshape the employment response to productivity growth. Under calibrated India frictions, agriculture settles at 35.9 percent of employment, against 26.4 percent in a near-frictionless economy.

2026
Whose Geopolitical Risk? Local Media and Producer Prices in Asia
Deeksha Kokas, Wai-Mun Chia
Preliminary draft — comments welcome
Monthly geopolitical risk indices built from domestic newspapers across seventeen Asian economies, in six languages, 1995–2026. A one-standard-deviation rise in local risk raises producer prices by roughly 0.8 percent over two years; the Anglosphere benchmark traces the same path without precision.

2026
Geopolitical Alignment and Trade Fragmentation: Asymmetric Effects of US- and China-Salient Risk
Deeksha Kokas, Taojun Xie, Huanhuan Zheng, Wai-Mun Chia
Working paper
Structural gravity estimates on continuous UN ideal-point distances show trade fragmenting along alignment lines, with China-salient risk generating more than three times the reallocation of US-salient risk. ASEAN-linked dyads show substantial attenuation, strongest in capital-intensive and value-chain goods.
